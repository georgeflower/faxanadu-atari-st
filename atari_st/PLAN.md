# Plan

The order follows the Zelda port. Each step is one or a few commits straight to `main` that build and are tested (in Hatari once there is something to see) and, when worth playing, get a disk image. The ROM harness (jsnes) is the reference: every behaviour is first written down from the disassembly, then compared with the harness, then built on the ST.

## Done

- [x] 2. ROM harness: jsnes plays the Europe ROM from RAM labels; scroll measured (64 frames of 4 px); labels checked.

## Order of work

The numbers are the order I intend to work in. A step is done when its tests pass and `AGENTS.md` says what was learned. **Unattended** means it can be done without the author: the harness gives the answer. **Needs the author** means a real machine, a choice or a file.

### A. Frame loop and the HUD (next)

- A1. **HUD that updates** (DONE, `src/hud.s`): HP, MP, experience, gold, time and the item box drawn from a state block by a routine, not from a fixed picture. The bars' partial tiles (8 values each, the magic bar with its halves swapped) come from the ROM. Test: Hatari picture equals `st_convert.hud_pixels` for several states (the five states of `hudshot.mjs`).
- A2. **The frame loop** (DONE, `src/frame.s`; two screens and a clean copy, decided in A3): VBL interrupt (50 Hz), one game tick per VBL, a double screen buffer or a drawn-under-the-beam scheme (decide after measuring), joystick and keyboard read through the IKBD (joystick port 1 as the NES pad; keyboard keys as an option), a frame counter. Test in Hatari with injected key events; measure cycles per frame with Hatari's profiler or a timer-B border colour.
- A3. **Sprite engine** (DONE: two screens, erase from the clean copy, the hero, the enemies, the spells and the people, behind the background as the NES has it, `en_blit`; no enemy scene is over a frame (`AGENTS.md` 5d, 5i). The NES drops or flickers nothing on these screens, so there is no per-line limit to copy. Not done, and not needed while the budget holds: poses composed once and shifted): draw and erase sprites over a still background (save-under or redraw of the touched blocks), the hero composed once per pose and shifted (see `AGENTS.md` 5e for the budget), a sprite list with the NES's priority rules and the per-frame limits the game has (`Sprites_SlotsFull`), "behind the background" for the hero, the enemies and the spells (done; Mist screen 3 with the hero and three Zozuras takes 66 to 71% of a frame, 95% before). Measure: hero + 8 sprites inside 60,000 cycles.

### B. Moving between screens (the display risk)

- B1. **Horizontal scroll between screens** (DONE, 16 px every 4 frames, tested in Hatari; still **needs the author** to judge on the real machine; original text: **needs the author** to judge on the real machine): 64 frames of 4 px = 16 px every 4 frames. Pre-compose the neighbour screen into a wider buffer and move the picture 16 px at a time with word-aligned copies; nothing else runs during the scroll (checked in the harness). Measure the copy cost (about 168 lines x 256 px x 4 planes = 21 KB a step, a few hundred thousand cycles if done naively: this is the main budget problem; options are a 4-step cache, or scrolling by redrawing only the new column of blocks and moving the rest with `movem`).
- B2. **Vertical scroll** (DONE, tested in Hatari for up and down; needs the author's eyes): the two playfields lie one after the other in memory and the window is copied from a moving line of them, 16 lines every 4 frames (the ROM moves 208 lines at 4 a frame; the ST's window is 200 high since G15). The camera (0 or 8 since G15; 0 to 40 before) moves only when the hero would leave the window (the author's report on Fax085; `AGENTS.md` 5d, `tests/test_camera.py`). Open: the 14 waiting frames show a still hero; the author to judge the camera's move on the real machine.
- B3. **Screen setup** [DONE: doors in the area and to other palette sets, buildings, out to other areas, edges between areas, the up-and-down ways of Trunk screens 12 and 22 (G15), keys, music per package; the scroll callers are covered by the scroll tests] (decided by the author, 6 Nov 2026: option 1, one area is loaded at a time, at the doors, with a pause at every area change): loading a screen from the area data on entry (block ids, tiles, palette from the door, neighbours), area changes, the other two `Screen_HandleScroll` callers, `Game_UpdatePlayerOnScroll`. Compare the first frame after every kind of move with jsnes.
- B4. **Palette per screen** [one palette per package, set from the door, DONE; enemy colours with D]: one 16-colour palette per palette set, set from the door's palette index; sprite palettes loaded on top by `Screen_LoadSpritePalette` (enemy colours) counted into the 16.

## Known risks

- Scrolling and the display size (step B): the biggest.
- Sprite cost on a machine with no blitter (A3).
- Colour: Faxanadu's per-area palettes against 16 colours, and enemy sprite palettes on top (B4).
- Data volume: 272 screens in eight areas and a lot of text. The maps are about 54 KB in the ROM's compressed form (about 200 bytes a screen; the biggest area, Mist, 17 KB); all tilesets together are 16 KB. Small enough to keep a whole area in memory, but sprite images (several variants each) and music add up: see G1.
- The script interpreter (E3): all NPC behaviour depends on it being exact.
- Bank 5 (music) and bank 12 (texts) differ between the US and Europe ROMs: the disassembly does not describe those bytes exactly.
- Only the author can run the real machine: plan G3 checks so each one is a short, specific question.
